Test Architecture

Total time: 11 - 14 minutes face-to-face interview with an examiner.

PART 1: Social Interaction 4-5 Mins

Answering questions about yourself, your life, hometown, and basic daily routines.

Goal: Fluent, natural answers using basic compound grammar.
PART 2: Individual Long Turn 3-4 Mins

1-to-2 minute monologue based on a Cue Card. You have 1 minute to prep and make notes.

Goal: Fluid narration, logical structuring, and thematic vocabulary.
PART 3: Two-way Discussion 4-5 Mins

Deep-diving into abstract issues conceptually connected to the topic introduced in Part 2.

Goal: Critical arguments, complex logical sequencing, and speculative vocabulary.

Band 7.0 Descriptors

What distinguishes a stable Band 7.0 speaker from a 6.0/6.5 speaker?

Fluency & Coherence (FC) Band 7.0

Speaks at length without noticeable effort or loss of coherence. Uses a wide range of connective markers (e.g., Admittedly, Having said that, Conversely) to transition thoughts.

Lexical Resource (LR) Band 7.0

Deploys less common vocabulary, collocations, and natural idiomatic expressions. Shows flexibility in paraphrasing when searching for words.

Grammatical Range (GRA) Band 7.0

Consistently uses a mix of complex structural features (Conditionals, Subjunctives, Passive voice, Relative clauses) with high structural accuracy.

Pronunciation (PR) Band 7.0

Uses systematic pronunciation features naturally (Intonation, Sentence Stress, and Chunking) to ensure effortless comprehension for the examiner.

High-Impact Handling Strategies

IELTS Chan Truong Methodology
PART 1

The "Answer + Paraphrase + Reason + Extension" Formula

To hit Band 7.0+, do not repeat the examiner's vocabulary. Instantly paraphrase the main key words and expand your point with a secondary contrast.

Question: "Do you prefer to study in the morning or in the evening?"

✓ Band 7.0+ Sample Answer:
"Without a doubt, I'm much more of a night owl than an early bird. I find myself most productive and sharp in the late hours of the night, mainly because there are fewer distractions. In contrast, mornings often leave me feeling a bit groggy and sluggish, so I usually reserve complex study tasks for the evening."
PART 2

The P.P.F Model & Comparison Advantage

The easiest way to speak smoothly for 2 full minutes without running out of ideas is to structure your story using a **Past - Present - Future** loop.

Past (Historical context) "Initially, I was highly skeptical about..." / "To tell the truth, back in my school days, I used to..."
Present (Current reality) "But as time went on..." / "Fast forward to today, and it has become an indispensable part of my daily routine..."
Future (Speculation) "Looking ahead, I reckon I will continue to..." / "If everything goes according to plan, I might..."
PART 3

The Advanced A.R.E.A Model

The examiner seeks abstract, macro-level thinking. Avoid personalized stories; instead, formulate logical, multi-faceted arguments.

Question: "Why do some people prefer to live in big cities?"

[A - Answer] "Undoubtedly, the primary draw of metropolitan areas is the sheer abundance of career opportunities."

[R - Reason] "This is largely because multi-national corporations and educational institutions are heavily concentrated in urban centers."

[E - Example] "For instance, in Vietnam, young graduates flock to cities like Hanoi or HCMC, as these hubs offer far more diverse career pathways."

[A - Alternative] "Conversely, living in the countryside, while peaceful, might severely limit one’s professional growth."

Rescue Phrases & Band-Boosting Idioms

Time-Buying Fillers (Avoiding Silent Pauses)

  • • "That's a rather fascinating question, and to be perfectly honest, I haven't given it much thought before..."
  • • "Off the top of my head, I’d say..." (Use when presenting an immediate intuitive thought)
  • • "Let me mull that over for a brief second..."

Requesting Clarification Politely

  • • "If I’m not mistaken, you are asking about... is that correct?"
  • • "Could you please clarify what you mean by the term [Keyword]?"

High-Scoring Natural Idioms

Once in a blue moon

Meaning: Very rarely.

"Since I started working, I only visit my hometown once in a blue moon."

A double-edged sword

Meaning: Having both positive and negative consequences (Perfect for Part 3).

"Emerging technology is definitely a double-edged sword..."

To hit the nail on the head

Meaning: To describe exactly what is causing a situation.

"You absolutely hit the nail on the head regarding urban issues..."
