Part 2: An Important Decision

Prompt

Describe an important decision you made that you were pleased with.

You should say:

  • what the decision was
  • when you made it
  • why you made that decision
  • what happened after you made it
  • and explain why you are pleased with this decision.

🔑 Suggested Vocabulary

Crucial crossroad /ˈkruː.ʃəl ˈkrɒs.rəʊd/ Ngã rẽ quan trọng
Weigh the pros /weɪ ðə prəʊz/ Cân nhắc được mất
Leap of faith /liːp əv feɪθ/ Nhắm mắt làm liều/tin tưởng
Ramifications /ˌræm.ɪ.fɪˈkeɪ.ʃənz/ Hệ quả lâu dài
In hindsight /ɪn ˈhaɪnd.saɪt/ Nhìn nhận lại vấn đề
Gut feeling /ɡʌt ˈfiː.lɪŋ/ Linh cảm, trực giác

Model Monologue (Band 7.5+)

[What the decision was & When]
"I'd like to talk about a highly significant choice I made approximately two years ago regarding my academic trajectory. Specifically, I decided to completely switch my university major from Finance to Graphic Design, which felt like a crucial crossroad in my life at the time."

[Why you made that decision]
"I had initially enrolled in Finance due to parental pressure and the perceived promise of a stable, lucrative career. However, after three semesters of grinding through spreadsheets and economic theories, I felt utterly miserable and intellectually unfulfilled. I spent weeks trying to weigh the pros and cons, fully aware of the long-term ramifications. Ultimately, I realized my true passion lay in visual arts, and I had to follow my gut feeling."

[What happened after & Why you are pleased]
"Taking that leap of faith was terrifying, and explaining it to my parents was an ordeal. However, almost immediately after transitioning, my academic performance skyrocketed because I was genuinely engaged in the coursework. Fast forward to today, I have already secured a part-time role at a creative agency. In hindsight, I am immensely pleased with this decision because it preserved my mental health and allowed me to align my career path with my authentic identity, rather than living out someone else's expectations."

Follow-up Questions

  • Was it difficult to make this decision?
    "Incredibly so. It involved confronting the fear of disappointing my family and the terrifying prospect of starting over from scratch academically."
  • Did anyone help you make it?
    "Yes, I confided extensively in a university career counselor. Her objective, unbiased guidance really helped me navigate my conflicting emotions."

Section 1: Everyday & Important Decisions

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Most mundane decisions, like choosing what to wear, are processed intuitively. However, daily decisions become arduous when they involve conflicting priorities or moral compromises. For instance, deciding whether to confront a colleague about a minor error forces one to balance the desire for workplace harmony against the necessity of operational efficiency, thereby significantly increasing the cognitive load required to make the choice."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The most pivotal decisions are those that permanently alter one's life trajectory. These universally include selecting a university major, deciding whether to relocate to a foreign country, and choosing a life partner. These are deeply consequential choices because they dictate one's financial security, geographical roots, and long-term emotional fulfillment."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Unquestionably. The advent of globalization and the digital revolution has granted modern individuals an unprecedented plenitude of options regarding careers, consumer goods, and even dating prospects. While this freedom is liberating, it often triggers the paradox of choice, leaving individuals paralyzed by anxiety over making the optimal decision among countless alternatives."

Section 2: Making Difficult Decisions

Band 8.0+ Answer: "It is inherently more prudent to solicit advice when confronting monumental choices. Human judgment is notoriously clouded by cognitive biases and emotional volatility. Consulting objective, experienced individuals provides critical external perspectives, effectively illuminating blind spots that one might otherwise overlook when ruminating in isolation."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "I strive to employ a symbiotic balance of both. I initially use logic to brutally assess the tangible facts, statistical probabilities, and financial implications. However, the final verdict must ultimately align with my emotional compass; a decision that appears perfectly rational on paper but feels inherently wrong on a visceral level usually leads to profound regret."

Section 3: Leadership and Decision-making

Band 8.0+ Answer: "While gathering diverse input is generally a hallmark of effective leadership, an absolute mandate to always consult others is counterproductive. In times of severe crisis where swift action is required, attempting to reach a democratic consensus can cause fatal delays. A competent leader must possess the acumen to discern when collaborative deliberation is beneficial and when unilateral, executive action is necessary."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The repercussions of incompetent leadership are typically catastrophic and cascading. In a corporate setting, a flawed strategic pivot can hemorrhage capital, leading to mass layoffs and systemic low morale. On a national scale, misguided socio-economic policies can destabilize entire institutions, plunge the populace into systemic poverty, and inflict far-reaching generational trauma."

Section 4: Band 8–9 Challenge Questions

Band 9.0 Answer: "In an increasingly complex world, anchoring monumental decisions in robust, empirical evidence is non-negotiable to mitigate catastrophic risk. Data provides an objective architecture that neutralizes inherent human biases. However, relegating decision-making entirely to algorithms is a reductive fallacy. Intuition—which is essentially the subconscious synthesis of decades of lived experience—remains indispensable for interpreting contextual nuances that raw data cannot quantify, particularly in ethically ambiguous or unprecedented scenarios."
Band 9.0 Answer: "Paradoxically, it has drastically amplified the difficulty. While technology has democratized access to information, effectively eliminating ignorance as an excuse, it has simultaneously spawned severe information overload. Individuals are now bombarded with an incessant stream of contradictory data points, expert opinions, and fake news. Consequently, the cognitive labor required to filter, verify, and ultimately synthesize this overwhelming volume of information frequently results in acute decision fatigue and paralyzing indecision."
