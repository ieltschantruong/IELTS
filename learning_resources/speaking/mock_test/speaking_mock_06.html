Part 2: Social Media Personality

Prompt

Describe a person you follow on social media.

You should say:

  • Who this person is
  • Which social media platform you follow them on
  • What kind of content they share
  • Why you started following them
  • And explain why you are interested in this person.

Model Monologue (Band 7.5+)

[Who & Which platform]
"I would love to talk about an exceptional content creator named Khoai Lang Thang, whom I have been avidly following on YouTube and Facebook for several years now. He is essentially a travel and lifestyle vlogger based in Vietnam, though his journeys frequently take him across the globe."

[What content & Why started]
"The primary reason I stumbled upon his profile was purely serendipitous; one of his videos about exploring rural cuisines was recommended to me by the YouTube algorithm while I was planning a domestic trip. Instead of showcasing superficial tourist traps, his content is deeply immersive. He shares exquisite documentaries that delve into indigenous cultures, local culinary secrets, and the heartwarming daily lives of ethnic minorities."

[Why interested]
"What truly makes him stand out—and the reason I remain so captivated by his work—is his incredibly authentic persona. Unlike many influencers who project an artificial, hyper-glamorous lifestyle, he maintains a humble, down-to-earth demeanor that genuinely resonates with his audience. Furthermore, his videos boast immense aesthetic appeal; his drone cinematography of natural landscapes is nothing short of breathtaking. Ultimately, watching his journeys continuously educates me about diverse cultures and constantly adds new, exciting destinations to my own personal bucket list."

Follow-up Questions

  • How often do you check this person’s posts?
    "Whenever he uploads a new long-form video, which is roughly once a week, I make it a point to watch it during my dinner time as a form of relaxation."
  • Have you ever recommended this person’s account to someone else?
    "Absolutely. I frequently forward his travel itineraries to my group of friends whenever we are brainstorming our next holiday destination."

Section 1: Social Media in Daily Life

Band 8.0+ Answer: "I would argue that the primary driver is the fundamental human need for validation and connection. Social media platforms ingeniously exploit this psychological trait by offering an unprecedented level of connectivity. It allows individuals to seamlessly network with peers globally, instantly consume highly tailored entertainment, and construct a digital identity that garners immediate, albeit often superficial, social approval through likes and shares."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Excessive consumption invariably leads to profound psychological detriments. Constant exposure to the meticulously curated realities of influencers breeds intense feelings of inadequacy and anxiety among users. Furthermore, it severely diminishes productivity and face-to-face social skills, essentially entrapping users in digital echo chambers where they only encounter opinions that reinforce their existing biases, limiting intellectual growth."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "This behavior is largely symptomatic of a modern cultural shift towards narcissism. Many individuals mistakenly conflate their self-worth with their online visibility. By broadcasting every mundane detail of their lives, they are perpetually seeking external validation to compensate for internal insecurities. Alternatively, some view it as a necessary tool for personal branding, monetizing their lifestyle to attract lucrative corporate endorsements."

Section 2: Technology and Relationships

Band 8.0+ Answer: "It has functioned as a double-edged sword. On one hand, video calling applications have successfully bridged the geographical divide, allowing diaspora families to maintain vital connections across continents. On the other hand, it has severely fractured intimacy during physical gatherings. It is increasingly common to see families sharing a dinner table while being entirely absorbed in their smartphones, resulting in a profound loss of genuine, undivided attention."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Categorically, no. While online messaging excels in logistical efficiency, it is completely devoid of crucial non-verbal cues—such as micro-expressions, physical touch, and vocal intonation. These tangible elements are the bedrock of human empathy. Consequently, attempting to resolve complex emotional conflicts or convey deep affection through text almost inevitably leads to misinterpretation and emotional detachment."

Section 3: Online Influence & Creators

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Their influence stems from the illusion of accessibility. Unlike traditional, aloof celebrities, influencers meticulously craft a relatable, 'girl-next-door' persona. By consistently sharing seemingly intimate details of their daily routines, they cultivate a powerful parasocial relationship with their audience. Followers begin to perceive them as trusted friends rather than corporate entities, making their lifestyle recommendations and product endorsements incredibly persuasive."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Undeniably. Given that they possess a platform capable of swaying the opinions of millions of impressionable youths, they must be held to a rigorous ethical standard. Promoting fad diets, unverified financial schemes, or unrealistic beauty standards constitutes a severe dereliction of moral duty. Platforms and regulatory bodies should actively penalize creators who exploit their audience's trust for deceitful monetary gain."

Section 4: Band 8–9 Challenge Questions

Band 9.0 Answer: "The boundary hasn't merely been blurred; it has been fundamentally eradicated. We now operate in an era of digital panopticism, where voluntarily surrendering intimate domestic moments is incentivized by algorithmic reward systems. This constant oversharing normalizes a severe infringement of privacy. Consequently, societal expectations have shifted; remaining private is paradoxically viewed with suspicion, compelling individuals to commodify their private spheres to maintain social relevance."
Band 9.0 Answer: "Stringent legislative regulation is absolutely imperative. Currently, tech conglomerates operate as autonomous monopolies, prioritizing lucrative user engagement over ethical moderation. This unregulated environment serves as a fertile breeding ground for rampant disinformation, coordinated cyberbullying, and the radicalization of political discourse. By imposing robust antitrust laws and holding platforms legally liable for malicious algorithms, governments can mitigate these societal threats while still preserving foundational rights to free speech."
