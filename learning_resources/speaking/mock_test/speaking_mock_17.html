Part 2: A Famous Person

Prompt

Describe a famous person you are interested in.

You should say:

  • who this person is
  • how you know about them
  • what sort of life they had or have
  • and explain why you are interested in this person.

Model Monologue (Band 7.5+)

[Who & How you know them]
"I would like to talk about Leonardo DiCaprio, who is unequivocally a household name across the globe. I first became aware of him years ago through his iconic role in 'Titanic', but over time, my knowledge of him has expanded far beyond his cinematic achievements by following his environmental activism on social media and reading news articles."

[What sort of life they had/have]
"Despite his current astronomical wealth and fame, he actually had rather humble beginnings, growing up in a rough neighborhood in Los Angeles. He began acting in commercials as a child to help support his family. Today, he leads a life that is constantly under the paparazzi's lens, but he masterfully navigates this by keeping his private life highly guarded. What truly defines his current life, however, is his role as a deeply committed philanthropist and environmentalist."

[Why you are interested in this person]
"The primary reason I find him so awe-inspiring isn't merely his charismatic acting skills, though those are undeniable. I deeply respect how he actively chooses to use his platform to amplify urgent issues like climate change and wildlife conservation. Instead of just enjoying a lavish Hollywood lifestyle, he is a true trailblazer who has donated millions of dollars and produced numerous documentaries to educate the public. His unwavering dedication to making the planet a better place makes him a public figure truly worth admiring."

Follow-up Questions

  • Would you like to be famous?
    "Honestly, no. While the financial security seems appealing, the complete loss of privacy and the constant public scrutiny would be far too stressful for me."
  • Have you ever met a famous person?
    "No, I haven't. I attended a massive concert once, but I was far too deep in the crowd to even get a glimpse of the singer up close!"

Section 1: The Concept of Fame

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Historically, fame was strictly reserved for actors, esteemed musicians, or prominent politicians. However, there has been a massive paradigm shift. Nowadays, 'influencers'—individuals who gain traction via TikTok or YouTube through gaming streams, makeup tutorials, or even just daily vlogs—frequently achieve a level of celebrity that rivals traditional movie stars."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "I attribute this to the illusion of an effortless, glamorous lifestyle peddled by social media. Young people are constantly exposed to influencers showcasing immense wealth, free sponsorships, and global adoration. This creates a deeply skewed perception where fame is equated with ultimate success and absolute freedom, masking the grueling work and lack of privacy behind the scenes."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Fame is undeniably a double-edged sword. On the positive axis, it provides vast economic privileges and a powerful platform to enact social change or promote altruism. Conversely, it entirely dismantles their anonymity. Mundane tasks like grocery shopping become logistical nightmares, and their interpersonal relationships are often strained by public speculation and fake friendships."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The most severe drawback is the constant, unrelenting scrutiny. Every minor misstep is magnified and permanently archived on the internet. Furthermore, there's a heavy psychological toll; constantly having to maintain a flawless public persona can lead to severe anxiety and a profound sense of isolation, despite being surrounded by millions of adoring fans."

Section 2: Celebrities as Role Models

Band 8.0+ Answer: "It's a highly subjective matter. Some celebrities are phenomenal role models, leveraging their wealth for philanthropy and demonstrating incredible work ethic. However, many public figures are famous purely for controversial behavior or superficial reality TV antics. Idolizing the latter can severely skew a child's moral compass, teaching them to prioritize attention over actual substance."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "They act as the absolute arbiters of modern culture. Through lucrative brand endorsements and highly curated Instagram feeds, celebrities dictate fashion trends globally. Unfortunately, this often fosters an unattainable standard of beauty and wealth, pressuring young people into mindless consumerism to emulate a lifestyle that is fundamentally a fabricated marketing strategy."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "I believe society frequently puts them on a pedestal that is impossible to maintain. They are fundamentally human and prone to errors. While they should certainly be held accountable for illegal or profoundly unethical actions, demanding absolute perfection 24/7 is both unrealistic and hypocritical on the part of the public."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Because their wealth and status are generated directly by public support, they possess an inherent civic duty to give back. They should utilize their immense platform to amplify marginalized voices, fund critical humanitarian causes, and responsibly fact-check information before disseminating it to millions of impressionable followers."

Section 3: The Media and Privacy

Band 8.0+ Answer: "The paparazzi frequently turn a celebrity's existence into a paranoid nightmare. The relentless pursuit for scandalous photographs means that public figures cannot even visit a hospital or drop their kids off at school without facing aggressive, flash-photography ambushes, which constitutes a severe invasion of privacy."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Absolutely. While they have willingly chosen a public-facing career, they have not forfeited their fundamental human rights. Their professional output—such as a movie or a concert—is public domain, but their domestic lives, medical history, and children's identities should remain legally protected sanctuaries."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "It stems from a complex mix of escapism and psychological projection. Reading about the glamorous, or alternatively, the disastrous private lives of the elite provides a temporary distraction from mundane reality. Furthermore, witnessing affluent individuals encounter universal human struggles—like heartbreak or illness—humanizes them, making the public feel a comforting, albeit twisted, sense of equality."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Unquestionably. Tabloid journalism thrives on controversy because outrage generates lucrative clicks. The media frequently orchestrates smear campaigns, stripping away context from a celebrity's actions to fabricate sensationalist narratives. This ruthless 'cancel culture' can permanently destroy reputations over minor infractions, displaying a terrifying lack of journalistic ethics."

Section 4: The Future of Fame

Band 8.0+ Answer: "It has completely democratized the process. Previously, powerful studio executives acted as gatekeepers. Today, anyone with a smartphone can engineer a viral moment and instantly amass millions of followers globally. However, this has also led to an era of ephemeral fame, where influencers are rapidly celebrated and discarded by fickle algorithms within weeks."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Yes, I believe fame will become increasingly fragmented and niche. Rather than possessing universally recognized 'megastars' like Michael Jackson, the future will likely consist of thousands of micro-influencers who cater to highly specific subcultures. Furthermore, the rise of AI-generated virtual influencers will blur the lines of celebrity entirely, forcing us to redefine what it means to be a public figure."

Section 5: Band 8–9 Challenge Questions

Band 9.0 Answer: "I strongly contend that celebrity culture functions as a glaring societal reflection. We currently elevate reality TV stars—individuals frequently devoid of any discernible artistic or intellectual merit—to astronomical fame simply because they embody the hyper-narcissism and consumerist greed that modern capitalism rewards. Our collective infatuation with these figures essentially proves that society currently prioritizes controversy and aesthetic superficiality over genuine, substantive talent."
Band 9.0 Answer: "Undeniably so. A profound danger emerges when respect morphs into a toxic cult of personality. When individuals unquestioningly accept the political opinions, medical advice, or moral stances of a celebrity simply because of their fame, they completely bypass their own analytical faculties. This mass outsourcing of critical thought creates a deeply vulnerable society, highly susceptible to manipulation by charismatic figures rather than being guided by empirical logic and intellectual autonomy."
