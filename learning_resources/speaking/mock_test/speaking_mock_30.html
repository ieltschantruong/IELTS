Part 2: An Environmental Law

Prompt

Describe a law that helps the environment.

You should say:

  • what the law is
  • how you learned about it
  • who benefits from it
  • and explain why you think this law is important for the environment.

Model Monologue (Band 7.5 - 8.5)

[What the law is & How you learned about it]
"I would like to talk about a highly progressive piece of legislation that was recently introduced in my country, which is the comprehensive ban on single-use plastics in all major supermarkets. The law explicitly prohibits retailers from providing thin plastic carrier bags to customers at checkout, forcing them to supply either paper bags, reusable fabric totes, or other biodegradable alternatives. I first learned about this environmental mandate through a massive public awareness campaign broadcasted on national television and heavily heavily promoted across social media platforms a few months before it was officially enacted."

[Who benefits from it]
"The beneficiaries of this law are truly multifaceted. In the short term, the local municipal councils benefit immensely, as it drastically reduces the sheer volume of non-degradable waste clogging up urban drainage systems, which previously caused severe localized flooding. Long-term, however, the ultimate beneficiary is the broader marine ecosystem. By cutting off the supply chain of plastic waste at the source, we are preventing millions of toxic microplastics from infiltrating our oceans and suffocating aquatic wildlife."

[Why it is important]
"I consider this law to be of paramount importance because it moves beyond mere suggestion and utilizes governmental power to penalize destructive behavior. Voluntary recycling programs have historically failed to mitigate ecological damage because convenience usually overrides conscience for most consumers. By ensuring this law is strictly enforced with heavy fines for non-compliant corporations, the state has forcefully initiated a paradigm shift in consumer habits. It effectively forces society to adapt to sustainable practices, which is an absolute necessity if we hope to restore some semblance of ecological equilibrium for future generations."

Follow-up Questions

  • Do you think people are happy to obey this law?
    "Initially, there was significant pushback due to the inconvenience. However, as people adapted and purchased their own reusable bags, the grumbling subsided, and the majority now recognize its necessity."
  • What happens if someone breaks an environmental law in your country?
    "For a corporate entity, the repercussions are severe financial penalties or even the revocation of their business license. For individuals, it usually involves steep fines, though enforcement at the grassroots level can unfortunately be quite lax."

Section 1: Environmental Laws & Government

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Individual civic action, while noble, is fundamentally insufficient to combat systemic environmental degradation. Only the state possesses the overarching legislative authority and macroeconomic resources necessary to implement regulations on a national scale. Furthermore, it is solely the government that can legally hold accountable massive industrial conglomerates whose corporate greed drives the majority of global pollution."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The primary obstacle is fierce lobbying from entrenched fossil fuel and manufacturing industries, who view environmental constraints as direct threats to their profit margins. Additionally, governments frequently face public pushback; implementing carbon taxes or restricting cheap, heavily polluting energy sources can precipitate short-term economic hardship and job losses for the working class, making such laws politically perilous to pass."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "I advocate for a hybrid 'carrot and stick' methodology. Severe, crippling financial penalties are non-negotiable to deter blatant ecological violations. However, relying solely on punishment creates a culture of evasion. To genuinely foster sustainable development, states must proactively subsidize and offer tax incentives to corporations that innovate and integrate green technologies, thereby making environmental stewardship a financially lucrative endeavor."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Effective enforcement mandates the eradication of bureaucratic corruption. Governments must establish fiercely independent environmental protection agencies equipped with robust funding and unhindered auditing powers. Utilizing advanced surveillance technologies, such as satellite imagery to detect illegal deforestation or drone monitoring for toxic effluent dumping, is also critical to bypass localized corruption and ensure strict compliance."

Section 2: Individual & Corporate Responsibility

Band 8.0+ Answer: "It must unequivocally be a symbiotic effort. It is a fundamental civic duty for individuals to alter their consumption habits—such as adopting plant-based diets or minimizing waste. However, placing the entire onus on the consumer is a corporate fallacy. Individuals cannot opt for eco-friendly public transit if the government refuses to build it. Therefore, the state must architect the sustainable infrastructure that allows individual civic responsibility to actually flourish."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "I attribute this to a combination of psychological distancing and immediate economic survival. The most devastating effects of climate change often feel abstract or geographically distant, failing to trigger an immediate sense of urgency. Furthermore, for impoverished demographics, prioritizing the long-term abstract health of the planet is a luxury they simply cannot afford when they are struggling to secure their immediate daily survival."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Corporations must fundamentally transition from a linear 'take-make-dispose' model to a circular economy. This involves radically redesigning products to ensure maximum longevity and absolute recyclability. Moreover, they must mandate transparency throughout their supply chains, ruthlessly auditing their third-party manufacturers to eradicate exploitative ecological practices, rather than simply engaging in superficial 'greenwashing' PR campaigns."

Section 3: Global Challenges

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Tragically, scientific consensus dictates that significant exacerbation is inevitable. Even if we halted all carbon emissions today, the greenhouse gases already trapped in the atmosphere guarantee decades of rising temperatures. Unless global superpowers instantly pivot to radical decarbonization—which seems highly improbable given current geopolitical gridlocks—we will witness an unprecedented escalation in extreme weather events and severe coastal flooding."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "It is absolutely the only viable solution. Ecological disasters inherently transcend sovereign borders; toxic emissions from a highly industrialized nation will inevitably acidify the oceans of neighboring, developing countries. Without legally binding, unified global treaties and profound international solidarity, isolated national policies will remain entirely futile against a planetary crisis."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Beyond the obvious ravages of extreme heatwaves, the most terrifying prospect for future generations is acute resource scarcity. The rapid depletion of freshwater aquifers and the exhaustion of arable topsoil will inevitably trigger catastrophic global food shortages. This ecological collapse is highly likely to precipitate unprecedented geopolitical warfare over access to basic survival resources."

Section 4: Band 8–9 Challenge Questions

Band 9.0 Answer: "Framing this as a binary choice is a fundamental socioeconomic fallacy. The illusion that we can possess infinite economic growth on a planet with finite biological resources has driven us to the precipice of extinction. While prioritizing the environment will undoubtedly cause immediate, severe disruptions to industries reliant on fossil fuels, it is a necessary triage. If we fail to strike a balance and allow irreversible damage to obliterate our ecosystems, the resulting global famine and societal collapse will instantly annihilate any short-term economic gains we previously prioritized."
Band 9.0 Answer: "Technology acts as an extraordinarily potent, neutral amplifier; its outcome is entirely dictated by the ethics of its deployment. Historically, industrial technology severely accelerated our catastrophic environmental decline. However, I posit that our salvation now exclusively relies on revolutionary tech. Advancements such as atmospheric carbon capture, lab-grown agriculture to reverse deforestation, and nuclear fusion are our only pragmatic lifelines. The existential caveat is whether we possess the collective political willpower to aggressively fund and democratize these green technologies before we cross the irreversible tipping point of ecological collapse."
