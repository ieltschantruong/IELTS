Part 2: A Building You Like

Prompt

Describe a building that you really like or dislike.

You should say:

  • What the building is
  • Where it is located
  • What it looks like
  • And explain why you like or dislike this building.

Model Monologue (Band 7.5+)

[What the building is & Where it is located]
"I would love to talk about Landmark 81, which is unarguably an architectural marvel and currently the tallest building in Vietnam. This towering skyscraper is prominently situated along the banks of the Saigon River, acting as the majestic centerpiece of the Vinhomes Central Park urban area in Ho Chi Minh City."

[What it looks like]
"In terms of its exterior, the building features an extraordinary contemporary design. The structure is heavily inspired by a traditional bundle of bamboo, which signifies strength and solidarity in Vietnamese culture. It consists of multiple staggered, glass-facaded tiers reaching into the clouds. At night, it is illuminated by a spectacular LED lighting system that completely mesmerizes anyone looking at the city skyline."

[Why you like this building]
"I am immensely fond of this building for a multitude of reasons. Firstly, it is incredibly aesthetically pleasing and serves as a proud symbol of Vietnam's rapid economic growth and modern ambition. Secondly, its functionality is unparalleled; it houses state-of-the-art facilities ranging from a luxury hotel and high-end shopping mall to premium residential apartments. Lastly, the observation deck near the pinnacle offers breathtaking, panoramic views of the entire sprawling metropolis, making it a perfect spot to unwind and appreciate the urban landscape."

Follow-up Questions

  • How often do you visit this building?
    "I visit it roughly once a month. My friends and I often frequent the commercial center at its base to catch the latest movie releases or dine at some of the exquisite restaurants located there."
  • Do you think this building is important to the local community?
    "Undoubtedly. Beyond being a monumental tourist attraction that injects substantial revenue into the local economy, it has effectively elevated the overarching prestige and real estate value of the entire surrounding district."

Section 1: Buildings and Architecture

Band 8.0+ Answer: "It presents a fascinating dichotomy based on location. In rural provinces, traditional low-rise dwellings constructed from brick and wood, often featuring terracotta tiled roofs, remain the ubiquitous standard. Conversely, rapid urbanization has radically transformed metropolitan areas into modern concrete jungles, where high-density commercial complexes and towering residential skyscrapers dominate the skyline to accommodate explosive population growth."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "I advocate for a highly selective approach to historic preservation. Structures that embody profound cultural narratives or showcase unparalleled architectural heritage must absolutely be safeguarded, as they serve as tangible links to our ancestral identity. However, blindly preserving every aging, dilapidated building is economically irrational. If an old structure poses severe safety hazards and lacks historical significance, replacing it with modern, utilitarian infrastructure is a necessary sacrifice for urban progress."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Architecture acts as a physical manifestation of a nation's core values, historical struggles, and geographical constraints. For instance, minimalist and highly functional designs in Scandinavian countries perfectly mirror their cultural emphasis on simplicity and harmony with nature. Similarly, grandiose, opulent cathedrals in Europe loudly communicate the profound historical influence and wealth of religious institutions. Ultimately, the materials chosen and the aesthetic motifs utilized are a direct translation of a society's collective ethos."

Section 2: Urban Development

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Urban planners grapple with a multitude of formidable hurdles, primarily centered around spatial limitations. In densely populated hubs, acquiring adequate land without triggering forced evictions and subsequent social unrest is incredibly contentious. Furthermore, integrating massive new structures without overburdening the existing, often antiquated, municipal grids—such as sewage systems and transportation networks—requires meticulous, costly logistical orchestration to prevent systemic collapse."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "In the context of rapidly expanding modern metropolises, building vertically is undeniably the more pragmatic strategy. Unrestricted outward expansion, or 'urban sprawl', inevitably encroaches upon vital agricultural lands and natural habitats, accelerating ecological degradation. Erecting vertical high-rises hyper-concentrates the population, which fundamentally optimizes the efficiency of public transit systems and minimizes the city's overall geographical footprint, thereby preserving surrounding green belts."

Section 3: Sustainable Buildings

Band 8.0+ Answer: "It is an absolute imperative in our fight against catastrophic climate change. The global construction and real estate sectors are notorious for consuming astronomical amounts of energy and generating a massive carbon footprint. By proactively integrating sustainable infrastructure—such as utilizing eco-friendly materials and implementing passive cooling designs—we can drastically curtail greenhouse gas emissions, significantly reduce reliance on fossil fuels, and ensure long-term ecological viability for future generations."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Undeniably. The transition toward green architecture is no longer merely an avant-garde trend; it is rapidly becoming a legislative mandate. As governmental bodies globally impose increasingly stringent environmental regulations and carbon taxes, developers will be financially compelled to adopt sustainable practices. Furthermore, corporate entities are actively seeking LEED-certified buildings to bolster their public relations, practically guaranteeing that eco-centric architecture will become the universal standard."

Section 4: Band 8–9 Challenge Questions

Band 9.0 Answer: "Achieving this equilibrium requires masterful, nuanced urban zoning. A city must not become a stagnant museum, yet simultaneously, it cannot ruthlessly bulldoze its past in the name of aggressive gentrification. The optimal strategy entails strictly designating core heritage districts where architectural integrity is legally immunized against commercial alteration. Conversely, authorities should allocate peripheral 'development zones' exclusively designated for high-density, modern infrastructure. This dual-pronged approach ensures that a metropolis can robustly compete in the global economy while reverently safeguarding the cultural soul that grants it unique identity."
Band 9.0 Answer: "The psychological impact of our built environment is profound and often entirely subconscious. Oppressive, brutalist structures devoid of natural light or communal spaces actively breed alienation and exacerbate urban depression. Conversely, enlightened architectural design—which deliberately integrates biophilic elements, maximizes solar exposure, and features accessible communal plazas—acts as a powerful catalyst for psychological restoration. By prioritizing human-centric design over mere spatial optimization, architects can literally engineer environments that passively alleviate stress and actively nurture organic social cohesion among residents."
