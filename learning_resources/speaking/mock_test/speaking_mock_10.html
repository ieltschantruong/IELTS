Part 2: An Inexpensive Day Out

Prompt

Describe a day out that didn't cost you much.

You should say:

  • Where you went
  • Who you went with
  • What you did
  • How much you spent
  • And explain why you enjoyed the day even though it was inexpensive.

Hidden gem /ˈhɪd.ən dʒem/ Nơi tuyệt đẹp ít người biết
Immerse in nature /ɪˈmɜːs ɪn ˈneɪ.tʃər/ Hòa mình vào thiên nhiên

Model Monologue (Band 7.5+)

[Where & Who]
"I'd like to share an experience about a fantastic day out I had just last month that was practically done on a shoestring budget. My best friend and I decided to escape the urban chaos and visit a local national park located about an hour's drive from the city center, which is somewhat of a hidden gem."

[What you did & How much you spent]
"Instead of splurging on an expensive restaurant lunch, we adopted a more frugal approach. We packed homemade sandwiches and some cold drinks. Once we arrived, we spent the entire morning hiking along a scenic trail, totally immersed in nature. We eventually found a quiet spot by a stream to lay down our picnic blanket. Excluding the negligible cost of the fuel and the homemade food, the only actual expense was the park entrance fee, which was merely three dollars each."

[Why you enjoyed it]
"Despite being incredibly cheap, the day was phenomenally rewarding. The physical exertion of the hike was exhilarating, and sitting by the water provided a profound opportunity to genuinely unwind. More importantly, the lack of digital distractions or flashy entertainment allowed us to engage in deep, meaningful conversations. It served as a powerful reminder that the best experiences in life are often driven by good company and a serene environment, rather than a hefty price tag."

Follow-up Questions

  • Do you often go on inexpensive trips?
    "Yes, quite frequently. As a student, I have to be mindful of my finances, so I actively seek out low-cost or free activities like visiting museums on their free admission days or hiking."
  • Do you think expensive trips are always more enjoyable?
    "Not necessarily. While luxury resorts offer unparalleled comfort, true enjoyment often stems from the people you are with and the novelty of the experience, which money cannot guarantee."

Section 5: Band 8–9 Challenge Questions

Band 9.0 Answer: "Consumerism asserts a profoundly pervasive influence, essentially leading to the commodification of leisure. Society is incessantly bombarded with advertising that equates joy with purchasing power—suggesting that a fulfilling weekend must involve expensive dining or acquiring luxury goods. This insidious narrative frequently distorts our perception, conditioning individuals to overlook the profound intrinsic joy found in free, simple pleasures like deep conversations or connecting with nature, effectively making happiness contingent on financial expenditure."
Band 9.0 Answer: "Yes, profoundly so. The architecture of modern digital life has fundamentally hijacked our dopamine receptors. Because we are continuously subjected to extreme, hyper-stimulating inputs—like endless algorithmic social media feeds and instant on-demand entertainment—our threshold for engagement has been artificially elevated. Consequently, slower, analog activities that once provided deep satisfaction, such as reading a physical book or quietly observing nature, now frequently register as mundane or under-stimulating to the modern brain."
Band 9.0 Answer: "Urban municipalities must prioritize equitable spatial planning. This involves aggressively reclaiming unused urban spaces to construct expansive, meticulously maintained public parks and robust cycling networks that are entirely free to utilize. Furthermore, local governments should heavily subsidize community centers that offer free artistic workshops, amateur sports leagues, and public cultural festivals, thereby democratizing access to high-quality recreation regardless of a resident's socio-economic status."
Band 9.0 Answer: "I strongly anticipate a significant paradigm shift towards the experiential economy. As societal awareness regarding sustainability and the ecological devastation caused by hyper-consumption grows, accumulating material wealth will likely lose its prestige. Furthermore, psychological research consistently proves that experiential purchases—such as traveling or learning a new skill—yield more enduring satisfaction and contribute far more to an individual's sense of identity than acquiring transient material goods."
