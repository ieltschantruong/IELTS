Part 2: A Memorable Journey

Prompt

Describe a memorable journey you have made.

You should say:

  • where you went
  • how you traveled
  • why you went there
  • and explain why it was memorable.

Model Monologue (Band 7.5+)

[Where & Why]
"I would love to recount a highly memorable trip I took last summer to Ha Giang, a remote mountainous province situated in the extreme north of Vietnam. I decided to embark on this journey primarily as a graduation reward to myself. After months of grueling academic pressure, I desperately needed to escape the urban chaos and explore somewhere completely off the beaten track."

[How you traveled]
"To fully experience the rugged terrain, I opted to travel by motorcycle along with a close group of friends. It was a gruelling three-day ride navigating through incredibly steep mountain passes and winding roads. While it was physically exhausting and occasionally terrifying due to the sharp cliff edges, riding a motorbike afforded us the unparalleled freedom to stop whenever we wished to capture the scenery."

[Why it was memorable]
"The journey was unforgettable for a multitude of reasons. First and foremost, the landscapes were genuinely breathtaking. Standing atop the Ma Pi Leng Pass and gazing down at the emerald Nho Que River snaking through the canyon induced a profound sense of tranquility. Furthermore, we had the opportunity to immerse ourselves in the local indigenous culture by staying at a traditional homestay. The sheer pristine beauty of the area combined with the genuine hospitality of the ethnic minority locals made it a once-in-a-lifetime experience that forever shifted my perspective on my home country."

Section 1: Travel Habits

Band 8.0+ Answer: "The motivations are highly multifaceted. On a fundamental level, travel acts as a potent form of escapism, allowing individuals to temporarily detach from the relentless pressure of their urban routines. Intellectually, it is the most effective method to broaden one's horizons. Encountering novel cultures, diverse culinary traditions, and drastically different socio-economic structures fosters profound empathy and dismantling of xenophobic prejudices."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Drastically so. Previously, international travel was an exclusive luxury reserved for the affluent elite. The advent of budget airlines and the proliferation of digital booking platforms have completely democratized the industry. Consequently, we have witnessed a massive paradigm shift from regimented, agency-led package tours to highly customized, independent backpacking, where travelers prioritize authentic, localized experiences over superficial sightseeing."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "It essentially depends on the demographic's socio-economic status. The burgeoning middle class and younger generations are increasingly drawn to international travel within Southeast Asia—like Thailand or Singapore—due to competitive airfares and a thirst for exoticism. Conversely, families with young children or elderly members still heavily favor domestic tourism, leveraging Vietnam's extensive coastline for pragmatic, logistical convenience."

Section 2: The Tourism Industry

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Tourism acts as an incredibly lucrative industry that can single-handedly resuscitate stagnating local economies. The massive influx of foreign capital directly stimulates grassroots entrepreneurship—spawning boutique hotels, artisanal craft markets, and independent tour operations. This rapid job creation often catalyzes municipal governments to heavily upgrade local infrastructure, such as hospitals and road networks, inadvertently elevating the living standards of the indigenous population."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Relying solely on tourism creates a highly volatile economic ecosystem. Because it is acutely vulnerable to external shocks—such as global pandemics, geopolitical instability, or even erratic weather—entire communities can plunge into sudden destitution if visitor numbers plummet. Furthermore, aggressive tourism development frequently inflates local property values, leading to severe economic disparity and effectively pricing out the original inhabitants from their own neighborhoods."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "State entities must deploy highly sophisticated, multi-channel digital campaigns. Utilizing global social media influencers and commissioning high-definition cinematic documentaries can dramatically showcase a nation's unique allure. Crucially, however, governments must simultaneously liberalize visa policies to eliminate bureaucratic friction. A brilliant marketing campaign is entirely futile if prospective tourists are deterred by labyrinthine and costly visa application processes."

Section 3: Cultural & Environmental Impact

Band 8.0+ Answer: "The impact is inherently paradoxical. While tourist revenue can fund the preservation of archaic monuments and endangered crafts, it frequently triggers rampant cultural commodification. Deeply sacred indigenous rituals or dances are often diluted and repackaged into brief, superficial spectacles explicitly designed to entertain foreign audiences, thereby stripping the culture of its profound historical gravitas and authenticity."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Unregulated mass tourism inflicts a colossal ecological footprint. The rapid construction of luxury resorts frequently obliterates fragile coastal ecosystems like mangroves. Moreover, an overwhelming influx of visitors inevitably leads to severe local resource depletion—particularly fresh water—and generates insurmountable volumes of plastic waste that municipalities are structurally ill-equipped to process, leading to severe marine and terrestrial pollution."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "It is a necessary conceptual shift, though challenging to execute flawlessly. Genuine eco-tourism—which strictly caps visitor numbers, utilizes renewable energy, and heavily reinvests profits into wildlife conservation—is highly effective. However, the term is frequently hijacked for 'greenwashing', where corporations falsely market conventional, destructive tours as eco-friendly to exploit conscientious consumers. Rigorous state regulation is mandatory to ensure the label genuinely mitigates environmental degradation."

Section 4: Transportation

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Trains offer a vastly superior aesthetic and psychological experience. Unlike the sterile, anxiety-inducing ordeal of airport security and cramped cabins, rail travel provides spacious seating and the luxury of watching picturesque landscapes unfold. Furthermore, an increasing demographic of eco-conscious travelers explicitly opts for high-speed rail to drastically minimize their carbon emissions associated with aviation."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The ramifications of chronic congestion are economically and biologically crippling. Economically, millions of productive man-hours are squandered daily in gridlock, severely impeding commercial logistics. Biologically, the concentrated exhaust fumes plunge air quality to hazardous levels, catalyzing a surge in respiratory illnesses among the urban populace and drastically lowering the overarching quality of city life."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "I envision a landscape dominated by absolute electrification and autonomy. The combustion engine will likely be rendered obsolete, replaced by sophisticated networks of autonomous electric vehicles (EVs) that communicate seamlessly with smart city grids to eliminate traffic jams. Furthermore, we may see the implementation of hyperloop technologies, effectively bridging distant metropolises in fractions of an hour and revolutionizing inter-city commutes."

Section 5: Band 8–9 Challenge Questions

Band 9.0 Answer: "It is an incredibly insidious, self-cannibalizing phenomenon. Overtourism essentially degrades the fundamental essence of a destination. Physically, the relentless foot traffic accelerates the structural decay of ancient monuments and devastates fragile ecosystems. Culturally, local inhabitants are frequently priced out of their historic centers due to the proliferation of short-term rentals, transforming vibrant, authentic neighborhoods into sterile, overcrowded amusement parks devoid of any genuine local soul."
Band 9.0 Answer: "Unquestionably, yes. As international travel becomes exponentially more accessible, destinations frequently dilute their unique characteristics to cater to the standardized expectations of Western tourists. You now witness identical multinational coffee chains and fast-fashion outlets dominating the high streets of almost every major global city. This rampant homogenization severely undermines cultural diversity, stripping away the idiosyncratic nuances that historically made traversing the globe an intellectually enriching endeavor."
Band 9.0 Answer: "This represents a profound ethical and economic conundrum. While the aviation sector is undeniably a massive contributor to global greenhouse gas emissions, arbitrarily capping flights would instantaneously cripple the economies of developing nations that rely almost exclusively on tourism for survival. A far more pragmatic and equitable approach would involve imposing stringent, globally standardized carbon taxes on aviation fuel, thereby utilizing market mechanisms to ruthlessly compel airlines to innovate and accelerate the development of sustainable, zero-emission propulsion technologies."
