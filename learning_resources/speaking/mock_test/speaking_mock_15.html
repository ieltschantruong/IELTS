Part 2: A River or Beach

Prompt

Describe a river or a beach that is important to your country or region.

You should say:

  • what it is
  • where it is located
  • what people can do there
  • why it is important to your country or region
  • and explain why you think it should be protected.

Model Monologue (Band 7.5+)

[What it is & Where it is located]
"I would love to talk about the Mekong River, which is undeniably one of the most prominent and geographically significant water bodies in Southeast Asia. This majestic, meandering river flows through several countries before finally culminating in a vast delta located in the southernmost region of Vietnam."

[What people can do there]
"The activities available along the delta are incredibly diverse. For locals, the river is a bustling hub of commerce; they frequently navigate small wooden boats to trade fresh produce at the famous floating markets. For tourists, it offers a deeply immersive cultural experience. Visitors can take guided boat tours through intricate canal networks, cycle along the shaded banks, or visit traditional craft villages that operate adjacent to the water."

[Why it is important & Why it should be protected]
"The Mekong River's importance cannot be overstated; it acts as the absolute agricultural lifeline for Vietnam. The nutrient-rich silt deposited by the river creates highly fertile soil, earning the delta its title as the 'rice bowl' of the nation. I firmly believe it must be fiercely protected due to its immense ecological significance. It is a critical biodiversity hotspot that sustains countless aquatic species. Unfortunately, it is currently facing severe threats from upstream dam construction and industrial pollution. Without robust, transnational conservation efforts, the degradation of this river will inevitably lead to catastrophic economic and environmental consequences for millions of people who depend on its ecosystem."

Follow-up Questions

  • Have you ever visited this place?
    "Yes, I've taken a few weekend excursions to the Delta. The vibrant energy of the floating markets at dawn is something I'll never forget."
  • Do many tourists visit this place?
    "Absolutely. It's a massive draw for international backpackers seeking an authentic, rural Vietnamese experience away from the modernized cities."

Section 1: Rivers, Beaches, and Water Resources

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Rivers function as the indispensable arteries of any nation. From a macroeconomic perspective, they provide crucial irrigation networks necessary to sustain large-scale agriculture, thereby ensuring national food security. Historically and contemporarily, they also serve as vital logistical routes, facilitating the cost-effective transportation of heavy commercial goods deep into inland territories."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "On a localized level, rivers frequently serve as the absolute backbone of the local economy. Communities situated along riverbanks heavily depend on them for artisanal fishing and aquaculture, which constitute their primary source of protein and income. Furthermore, aesthetically pleasing waterways act as magnets for regional eco-tourism, generating a steady stream of supplementary revenue for local service providers."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Beaches offer a highly sought-after multi-sensory escape from the suffocating pressure of urban environments. The rhythmic sound of ocean waves combined with expansive, unobstructed horizons induces profound psychological relaxation. Additionally, they cater to diverse demographics, offering tranquil sunbathing for those seeking rest, alongside rigorous water sports for thrill-seekers, making them universally appealing."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The recreational options are immensely varied. At beaches, tourists frequently engage in surfing, snorkeling to observe coral reefs, or simply lounging on the sand. Conversely, rivers tend to attract enthusiasts of freshwater kayaking, leisurely boat cruises, or recreational angling, offering a distinctly different, yet equally engaging, connection with aquatic environments."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The environmental temperaments differ drastically. Coastal living boasts a dynamic, breezy atmosphere heavily influenced by tidal shifts and saline air, which, while refreshing, often accelerates the corrosion of infrastructure. Riverine living, however, typically offers a more placid, sheltered environment, though it inherently carries a significantly higher risk of devastating seasonal flooding during monsoon periods."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "This is a fundamental pattern driven by human survival and economic pragmatism. Before the advent of modern plumbing, immediate access to fresh water was non-negotiable for sanitation and agricultural irrigation. Furthermore, rivers acted as natural, frictionless highways for early trade networks, allowing settlements to rapidly evolve into affluent commercial hubs by dominating regional logistics."

Section 2: Water and the Environment

Band 8.0+ Answer: "The primary culprits are aggressive industrialization and negligent agricultural practices. Manufacturing facilities frequently discharge untreated, highly toxic chemical effluents directly into adjacent waterways. Compounding this, excessive use of synthetic fertilizers on farmlands inevitably leads to toxic agricultural runoff during heavy rains, which severely disrupts the ecological equilibrium of downstream marine environments."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The ramifications are immediate and devastating. From a public health standpoint, consuming or interacting with contaminated water acts as a rapid vector for severe gastrointestinal and dermatological diseases. Economically, when marine ecosystems deteriorate, it utterly decimates local fisheries and cripples coastal tourism industries, plunging entire communities into sudden financial destitution."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "While systemic change requires governmental action, individuals possess immense collective power. A critical step is fiercely boycotting single-use plastics to prevent microplastic accumulation in oceans. Furthermore, citizens must adopt responsible domestic practices, such as exclusively using biodegradable household cleaning agents and ensuring toxic substances like motor oil or pharmaceuticals are never poured down domestic drains."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "It is an absolute, non-negotiable imperative. Corporations left unchecked will perpetually prioritize profit margins over environmental stewardship. Governments must architect stringent regulations carrying punitive financial fines that far exceed the cost of proper waste management, thereby eliminating the economic incentive for illegal industrial dumping."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The core issue is a terrifying imbalance in supply and demand. Exponential global population growth is rapidly depleting existing freshwater reserves, while rampant industrial pollution simultaneously contaminates the limited remaining sources. This accelerating scarcity threatens to trigger severe geopolitical conflicts over water rights in the near future."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Effective stewardship demands legally binding, transnational treaties. Countries sharing extensive river basins must establish joint regulatory commissions to transparently monitor water extraction limits and enforce collective anti-pollution protocols. Ignoring a shared symbiotic relationship with cross-border waterways will inevitably lead to devastating downstream ecological collapse."

Section 3: Tourism and Water

Band 8.0+ Answer: "The impact is inherently paradoxical. Carefully managed eco-tourism can generate vital revenue directly funding local conservation efforts. Band 8.0+ Answer: "From a purely socio-economic vantage point, the benefits are monumental. Developing a robust coastal tourism infrastructure rapidly stimulates local economies, creating thousands of jobs in the hospitality, transport, and retail sectors. Band 8.0+ Answer: "The rapid commercial exploitation required to sustain mass tourism often necessitates the aggressive clearing of mangrove forests and dunes to construct luxury resorts. Band 8.0+ Answer: "Unquestionably. Certain ecological zones, such as fragile coral reefs or endangered breeding grounds, are simply too delicate to withstand constant human interference. Implementing strict access bans or highly limited, heavily supervised visitation quotas is an absolutely necessary, pragmatic step to prevent irreversible ecological collapse."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Tourists must transition from passive consumers to conscientious stewards. This entails strictly adhering to 'leave no trace' principles, deliberately patronizing eco-certified local businesses, and utilizing reef-safe sunscreens. Respecting local environmental guidelines is not a courtesy; it is a fundamental obligation when visiting delicate marine habitats."

Band 8.0+ Answer: "This is a highly contentious proposition. Governments must firmly abandon the pursuit of unsustainable mass tourism and instead implement strict visitor quotas alongside hefty ecological preservation taxes.
