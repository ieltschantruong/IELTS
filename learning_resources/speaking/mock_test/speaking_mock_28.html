Part 2: A Friend's Home

Prompt

Describe a friend's home you visited that you liked, but would not want to live in.

You should say:

  • whose home it is
  • where it is located
  • what the home looks like
  • and explain why you liked it but would not want to live there.

Model Monologue (Band 7.5 - 8.5)

[Whose home & Where it is located]
"I'd like to tell you about a rather unique property owned by a former university colleague of mine, Mark. A few months ago, he invited me over for a weekend getaway. His home is a renovated farmhouse located practically in the middle of nowhere, situated deep within a rural valley about three hours away from the city."

[What the home looks like]
"Aesthetically, the place is an absolute dream. It exudes an incredible rustic charm. The exterior features exposed brickwork and a sprawling wrap-around wooden porch overlooking acres of pine trees. Inside, it boasts high vaulted ceilings with massive timber beams, a traditional stone fireplace, and floor-to-ceiling windows that flood the living room with natural light. The cozy ambiance makes it feel like a luxury retreat straight out of a lifestyle magazine."

[Why you liked it but wouldn't live there]
"I thoroughly enjoyed my brief stay because the environment was incredibly secluded and peaceful, providing a perfect digital detox and a much-needed break from the urban noise. However, actually residing there permanently would be highly impractical for me. Firstly, the commute to my corporate job in the city would be a complete logistical nightmare, draining hours of my day. Secondly, the lack of immediate infrastructure—like reliable high-speed internet, supermarkets, or nearby hospitals—induces a sense of profound isolation. Lastly, keeping such a massive, aging wooden property clean and functional is notoriously high-maintenance. So, while it's an idyllic vacation spot, it entirely contradicts my need for modern urban convenience."

Follow-up Questions

  • Would you consider living there if you were retired?
    "Perhaps. Once I am freed from the obligation of a daily commute, the tranquility might become highly appealing, provided my healthcare needs could still be met."
  • Do many young people in your country prefer living in the countryside?
    "Generally, no. The vast majority of the youth actively migrate toward metropolitan hubs in pursuit of lucrative career opportunities and vibrant social lives."

Section 1: City vs. Countryside Living

Band 8.0+ Answer: "The primary magnet for the youth is economic opportunity. Metropolitan areas function as the epicenters of commerce, offering vastly superior career trajectories and higher remuneration compared to stagnant rural economies. Furthermore, cities provide unparalleled social dynamism; the sheer concentration of entertainment venues, diverse culinary scenes, and networking events caters perfectly to a young adult's desire for a vibrant lifestyle."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The most glaring detriment is the severe lack of infrastructure. Rural inhabitants frequently suffer from inadequate access to specialized healthcare, robust public transit, and high-speed internet. Additionally, while the tranquility is appealing, the profound isolation can lead to a stifling lack of cultural stimulation or intellectual diversity, making it an unappealing prospect for ambitious individuals."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "It is highly probable. As urban populations surge, the demand for housing pushes city boundaries aggressively outward, converting former agricultural lands into suburban commuter belts. Unless governments implement stringent vertical zoning laws to densify existing urban cores, this relentless horizontal expansion will inevitably continue, drastically increasing commuting times and environmental degradation."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The contrast is absolute. Cities are essentially concrete jungles plagued by acute noise and air pollution, where artificial lighting completely obscures the night sky. The countryside, conversely, retains a symbiotic relationship with nature; it boasts pristine air quality, abundant green acreage, and a profound silence that allows for genuine psychological decompression."

Section 2: Architecture and House Design

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Older properties possess an inherent, irreplaceable character. People are drawn to the bespoke craftsmanship, high ceilings, and historical narratives embedded within the architecture—elements completely absent in sterile, mass-produced modern complexes. Furthermore, traditional houses often come with expansive land or gardens, granting a degree of privacy that high-density apartments fundamentally cannot provide."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The psychological impact of interior design is profound. A house with abundant natural light, seamless ventilation, and open communal spaces organically fosters a sense of vitality and familial connection. Conversely, poorly lit, cramped, and heavily compartmentalized layouts can induce feelings of claustrophobia and subtly exacerbate underlying stress or lethargy in the inhabitants."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Undeniably. Minimalism has surged in popularity as a direct psychological rebellion against modern consumerist fatigue. By stripping a living space of superficial clutter and retaining only highly functional or deeply cherished items, individuals can cultivate an environment of stark clarity. It transforms the home into a serene sanctuary that visually and mentally counteracts the overwhelming chaos of the outside world."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "A balanced regulatory framework is crucial. While governments shouldn't dictate subjective interior aesthetics, they absolutely must enforce stringent mandates regarding structural integrity, fire safety, and energy efficiency. Furthermore, zoning laws are necessary to ensure that exterior designs maintain a cohesive urban aesthetic and do not aggressively clash with the surrounding historical or cultural environment."

Section 3: Moving Houses

Band 8.0+ Answer: "This nomadic lifestyle is largely symptomatic of a hyper-fluid modern economy. Young professionals often relocate to aggressively chase lucrative career opportunities or pursue specialized education across different cities. Additionally, the exorbitant cost of modern rent frequently forces tenants to migrate to cheaper districts as aggressive gentrification prices them out of their current neighborhoods."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "It entirely depends on one's stage of life. Renting offers unparalleled flexibility, making it ideal for young adults whose career trajectories are still volatile, as it frees them from the burden of property maintenance. However, entering the property ladder and buying a home is ultimately superior for long-term wealth accumulation, transforming a monthly housing expense into a permanent, appreciating asset."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "It is a profoundly disruptive event. Children thrive on predictability; uprooting them severs their established peer networks and familiar school environments, which can trigger intense anxiety or temporary academic regression. However, if managed empathetically by parents, surviving the transition can drastically bolster a child's adaptability and foster robust social resilience in the long run."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The physical logistics are notoriously grueling—packing, transporting heavy furniture, and navigating labyrinthine bureaucratic paperwork for changing addresses. But the most underestimated difficulty is psychological. Re-establishing a new sense of community from scratch, locating trusted local amenities, and acclimating to a foreign neighborhood dynamic requires a highly exhausting period of adjustment."

Section 4: Band 8–9 Challenge Questions

Band 9.0 Answer: "I assert that the crisis is predominantly a spectacular failure of state intervention. While the massive influx of rural populations into urban centers is an inevitable economic trend, the severe scarcity of affordable housing is entirely man-made. Governments have consistently capitulated to powerful real estate conglomerates, prioritizing lucrative luxury developments while utterly failing to construct adequate social housing. Furthermore, archaic zoning laws often prohibit high-density construction in key areas, artificially restricting supply and driving prices to astronomically unsustainable levels."
Band 9.0 Answer: "Remote work will catalyze an aggressive decentralization of the workforce. By decoupling employment from a physical office, professionals are no longer tethered to exorbitantly expensive metropolitan cores. This will precipitate a massive exodus towards 'tier-two' cities or rural peripheries, where individuals can secure vastly superior property dimensions for a fraction of the cost. Consequently, central business districts will suffer severe commercial devaluation, forcing urban planners to radically repurpose abandoned office high-rises into hybrid residential or recreational hubs to stave off urban decay."
Band 9.0 Answer: "Paradoxically, absolute physical proximity often breeds profound psychological disconnect. In towering residential blocks, the sheer volume of transient neighbors makes organic trust-building impossible. To protect their privacy amidst such overwhelming density, individuals inherently adopt a defensive posture of anonymity, actively avoiding eye contact in elevators or hallways. Thus, while hundreds of families may reside within a single building, the architectural structure itself systematically suppresses the formation of any genuine, interwoven sense of community."
Band 9.0 Answer: "It is fundamentally a pragmatic symptom disguised as a cultural evolution. While developers aggressively market co-living as a utopian method to cure urban loneliness and foster networking, the underlying reality is brutally economic. The staggering inflation of urban rent has essentially eradicated the possibility of solo living for young professionals. Therefore, co-living is less a spontaneous desire for communal harmony and more a forced, structural necessity where individuals must surrender their privacy simply to afford a roof over their heads."
