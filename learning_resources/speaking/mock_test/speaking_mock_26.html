Part 2: A TV/Online Programme

Prompt

Describe a TV or online programme that you enjoy watching.

You should say:

  • what the programme is
  • when and where you watch it
  • what it is about
  • and explain why you enjoy watching it.

Model Monologue (Band 7.5 - 8.0)

[What the programme is & When/Where you watch it]
"I would absolutely love to talk about a spectacular nature documentary series produced by the BBC, titled 'Planet Earth II'. I typically stream it on Netflix in the comfort of my own living room. Since my weekdays are often chaotic, I usually reserve my Sunday evenings to sit back, unwind, and binge-watch a couple of episodes."

[What it is about]
"Essentially, the program is a sweeping, visually stunning exploration of global biomes. Each episode focuses on a distinct habitat—ranging from dense, remote jungles and scorching deserts to the surprisingly thriving ecosystems found within modern cities. It is masterfully narrated by Sir David Attenborough, whose iconic, soothing voice adds an incredible layer of gravitas to the footage. The overarching theme is the sheer resilience of wildlife surviving against insurmountable odds."

[Why you enjoy watching it]
"There are several profound reasons why I am so enamored with this series. Firstly, the breathtaking cinematography is unparalleled; the camera crews use cutting-edge technology to capture intimate animal behaviors that have never been filmed before, creating a deeply immersive experience. Secondly, unlike superficial reality TV, this program possesses immense educational value. It seamlessly blends a captivating narrative with crucial environmental messages, subtly raising awareness about climate change and habitat destruction without ever feeling overly preachy. It is a genuine cinematic masterpiece."

Follow-up Questions

  • Would you recommend this programme to others?
    "Without a shadow of a doubt. I've already urged several friends to watch it. Its universal appeal makes it suitable for all demographics, from young children to the elderly."
  • Do you prefer watching TV alone or with others?
    "For a complex documentary like this, I strictly prefer watching alone. It demands undivided attention, and I find that chatting during the episode ruins the immersive atmosphere."

Section 1: Viewing Habits

Band 8.0+ Answer: "The motivations for media consumption are highly multifaceted. For the vast majority, television acts as a low-friction form of escapism, providing immediate psychological relief after a grueling workday. However, beyond mere entertainment, a significant demographic utilizes broadcasting to stay informed about global events, relying on news networks to concisely disseminate complex geopolitical developments."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "In Vietnam, reality talent shows and lengthy family-centric soap operas heavily monopolize prime-time viewing. These genres resonate deeply with the general populace because they frequently mirror traditional cultural dynamics and offer highly relatable, emotionally engaging narratives that appeal across multiple generations."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "We have witnessed a monumental paradigm shift regarding how content is consumed. Historically, families would gather around a single television set at specific hours to watch scheduled broadcasts. Today, with the proliferation of smartphones and high-speed internet, viewing has become highly individualized. People now expect on-demand access, allowing them to binge-watch entire series during their commutes rather than adhering to rigid network schedules."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Absolutely. Discussing trending shows is a ubiquitous social lubricant. Whether it's dissecting a controversial plot twist in a popular drama or analyzing a contestant's performance in a reality show, these shared media experiences foster communal bonding and provide safe, neutral ground for daily conversation."

Section 2: Children and Educational Media

Band 8.0+ Answer: "Unquestionably. When carefully curated, television is a potent pedagogical tool. High-quality educational programming can vividly illustrate complex scientific concepts or historical events that traditional textbooks struggle to convey. However, the caveat is that this consumption must be strictly monitored by guardians to prevent exposure to inappropriate content."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "I strongly advocate for a balanced hybrid approach. If a program is exclusively academic, it will inevitably fail to retain a child's notoriously short attention span. The most effective children's media seamlessly intertwines foundational learning—such as literacy or empathy—with highly engaging, entertaining narratives, thereby delivering subliminal messaging that fosters both cognitive and emotional development."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The detriments of unmoderated screen time are severe. Physically, it actively promotes a highly sedentary lifestyle, directly contributing to childhood obesity. Psychologically, prolonged exposure to rapidly editing, hyper-stimulating visuals can fundamentally rewire a developing brain, significantly impairing their ability to focus on slower, analog tasks like reading or engaging in imaginative play."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "The onus falls squarely on the parents. While broadcasting networks have a legal obligation to enforce age-appropriate censorship during daytime hours, they cannot act as a surrogate parent. Guardians must proactively utilize parental control software and actively co-view content to contextualize complex themes, ensuring their children develop healthy media literacy."

Section 3: Traditional TV vs Online Streaming

Band 8.0+ Answer: "It is highly probable that traditional broadcast TV will eventually become obsolete, much like the telegraph. Streaming platforms offer an unprecedented level of autonomy, allowing viewers to curate their own schedules rather than conforming to a network's rigidity. The only domain where traditional TV currently retains a stronghold is live sports broadcasting and breaking news."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "This preference is largely prevalent among older demographics who value the simplicity of a curated experience. With traditional TV, the viewer doesn't suffer from 'decision fatigue'; they simply turn the device on and consume whatever is presented. Furthermore, watching a live broadcast simultaneously with millions of others creates a subtle sense of community that isolated streaming lacks."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Algorithms exert a profoundly insidious influence. By relentlessly analyzing a user's viewing history, these platforms feed them a continuous loop of homogenous content designed to maximize retention. While this guarantees user satisfaction, it essentially traps individuals in a cultural echo chamber, severely restricting their exposure to diverse genres or opposing viewpoints."
Band 8.0+ Answer: "Exponentially so. Traditional TV commercials employ a 'scattergun' approach, broadcasting generically to massive, diverse audiences with low conversion rates. Online platforms, however, utilize hyper-targeted marketing based on granular user data. They deliver highly personalized advertisements directly to demographics that are statistically proven to be receptive, guaranteeing a vastly superior return on investment for corporations."

Section 4: Band 8–9 Challenge Questions

Band 9.0 Answer: "I assert that the risk of cultural homogenization is critically high. When major streaming platforms dominated by Western narratives achieve global ubiquity, they inherently export a specific set of cultural norms, aesthetic standards, and linguistic colloquialisms. This pervasive 'soft power' can easily overshadow indigenous storytelling. Consequently, local youth may begin to internalize and emulate foreign ideals over their own ancestral heritage, resulting in a tragic dilution of global cultural diversity."
Band 9.0 Answer: "It undeniably fractures the communal fabric. Historically, monumental television broadcasts acted as a unifying cultural touchstone; the entire nation would tune in simultaneously, providing a universal topic of conversation the following day. Today, hyper-individualized algorithms ensure that family members sitting in the same room are consuming entirely disparate, isolated media diets. This severely diminishes our shared cultural references, subtly eroding the cohesive narratives that bind a society together."
Band 9.0 Answer: "It is an absolute necessity for preserving national identity. Global conglomerates possess astronomical production budgets that domestic creators simply cannot rival. If governments do not intervene by enforcing domestic content quotas or providing substantial subsidies to local filmmakers, the indigenous media landscape will inevitably collapse. Protecting the local arts sector isn't merely about economic protectionism; it is about defending the sovereign right to tell one's own national story."
Band 9.0 Answer: "This represents arguably the most profound existential threat to modern democracy. When algorithmic curation prioritizes user engagement by exclusively feeding them content that validates their preconceived ideologies, it entirely dismantles objective reality. 'Truth' ceases to be a universal, empirical benchmark and morphs into a subjective, fragmented experience. This extreme polarization makes constructive societal discourse practically impossible, as differing demographics are literally operating from completely incongruent sets of 'facts'."
