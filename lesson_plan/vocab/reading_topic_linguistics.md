# **IELTS Vocabulary: Linguistics & Education**

**Topic 5: Linguistics & Education**

**Curated by: IELTS Chan Truong**

### **1\. Acquisition**

* **Word/Phrase**: Acquisition  
* **IPA & Audio**: /ˌæk.wɪˈzɪʃ.ən/ 🔊  
* **Part of Speech**: n.  
* **CEFR Level**: C1  
* **Definition (English)**: The process of getting or learning something, especially a new language or skill.  
* **Vietnamese Translation**: Sự tiếp thu, sự lĩnh hội  
* **Usage Explain**: Extremely common in passages discussing how infants learn their first language ("language acquisition").  
* **Example Sentence**: Theories of child development often focus on the rapid rate of language acquisition during the first three years of life.  
* **Word Family**: acquire (v.), acquisitive (adj.)

### **2\. Pedagogy**

* **Word/Phrase**: Pedagogy  
* **IPA & Audio**: /ˈped.ə.ɡɒdʒ.i/ 🔊  
* **Part of Speech**: n.  
* **CEFR Level**: C1  
* **Definition (English)**: The method and practice of teaching, especially as an academic subject or theoretical concept.  
* **Vietnamese Translation**: Phương pháp giảng dạy, sư phạm  
* **Usage Explain**: Used to describe the theories behind how teachers teach and how students learn.  
* **Example Sentence**: The university is reviewing its pedagogy to incorporate more interactive and student-centered learning methodologies.  
* **Word Family**: pedagogical (adj.), pedagogically (adv.), pedagogue (n.)

### **3\. Bilingualism**

* **Word/Phrase**: Bilingualism  
* **IPA & Audio**: /baɪˈlɪŋ.ɡwəl.ɪ.zəm/ 🔊  
* **Part of Speech**: n.  
* **CEFR Level**: B2  
* **Definition (English)**: The fact of being able to use two languages equally well, or the use of two languages in a particular area.  
* **Vietnamese Translation**: Song ngữ, sự thông thạo hai ngôn ngữ  
* **Usage Explain**: Often appears in texts debating the cognitive benefits or brain plasticity associated with speaking two languages.  
* **Example Sentence**: Recent neurological studies suggest that lifelong bilingualism can delay the onset of dementia.  
* **Word Family**: bilingual (adj./n.), bilingually (adv.)
